TOPIC: Daily protein guidance
CONTENT: For generally healthy adults, many nutrition education sources discuss roughly 0.8 g protein per kg body weight as a common baseline. People aiming for muscle gain often target higher intakes from food, commonly discussed around 1.2–2.0 g/kg depending on training and total calories. These are approximate educational ranges, not medical prescriptions.

TOPIC: Soya chunks for muscle gain
CONTENT: Soya chunks (textured soy protein) are a high-protein, low-fat vegetarian food. Per 100 g dry soya chunks, protein is commonly listed around 50 g. When cooked they absorb water and volume increases, so cooked protein density per 100 g is lower. They are affordable and useful for muscle-gain style vegetarian meal plans when calories and overall diet are adequate.
SOURCE: Indian food composition style tables / educational nutrition notes

TOPIC: Paneer nutrition
CONTENT: Paneer is a common Indian dairy protein food. Per 100 g, approximate values often cited are about 265 kcal, 18 g protein, and higher fat content. It is suitable for vegetarian and eggetarian diets but not for milk-allergic or vegan users. Portion control matters for calorie-focused goals.
SOURCE: Common Indian dairy nutrition references (approximate)

TOPIC: Affordable high-protein vegetarian foods
CONTENT: Budget-friendly high-protein vegetarian options in India commonly include soya chunks, dal/lentils, chickpeas, rajma, curd, paneer (moderate budget), oats, and besan. Combining cereals with legumes improves overall amino acid balance across the day.
SOURCE: Educational Indian diet planning notes

TOPIC: Weight loss meal pattern
CONTENT: Educational weight-loss meal patterns usually emphasize a calorie deficit, higher protein to support satiety, plenty of vegetables, and controlled oil/fried snacks. Extreme restriction, detox teas, or unsafe fasting advice should be avoided. Individual medical conditions require professional care.
SOURCE: General healthy-eating education

TOPIC: Muscle gain meal pattern
CONTENT: Educational muscle-gain patterns usually combine resistance training with a modest calorie surplus and protein-rich meals spread across the day. Indian examples include soya chunks, eggs (if diet allows), paneer, dal-rice, chicken, fish, oats, and curd. Food quality and total protein matter more than any single superfood.
SOURCE: General sports nutrition education (non-clinical)

TOPIC: Allergy safety
CONTENT: Always exclude foods matching a user's stated allergies (peanuts, milk, soy, gluten, eggs, or other). Cross-contact can occur in kitchens; educational meal plans cannot guarantee allergen-free preparation.
SOURCE: Food allergy safety education
